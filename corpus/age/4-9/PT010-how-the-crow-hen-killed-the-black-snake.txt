How the Crow-Hen Killed the Black Snake

There was a big banyan tree. A crow and his wife lived in it.

A black snake lived inside the tree. He ate the crow's chicks as soon as they were born.

The crow was very sad. He couldn't leave the tree he loved.

His wife, the crow-hen, said, "Let's move to a safer tree. Our children are dying."

The crow was worried. "We've lived here a long time. What if we can't find a new home?"

The crow-hen said, "We can't stay here. It's not safe for our children."

The crow thought, "I'll find a way to kill the snake. My friends will help me."

He went to a jackal who lived in another tree. The crow told the jackal about the snake.

The jackal said, "The snake is already in trouble. He's mean and cruel. He'll fall into trouble by himself."

The crow asked, "How will that happen?" The jackal told a story.

A heron ate fish every day. He ate good fish and bad fish. But he got too greedy and died.

The crow asked, "How will the snake die?" The jackal said, "Go to a place where a king lives. Take a golden chain from a rich man who is not watching. Drop it in the snake's hole. When the king's men come to get it back, they will kill the snake."

The crow and his wife flew away. The crow-hen found a pond. The king's women were playing in the water. Golden chains lay on the bank. She took one chain and flew home.

The king's men saw her. They ran after her with clubs. The crow-hen dropped the chain in the snake's hole. Then she waited far away.

The king's men climbed the tree. They found the snake in the hole. They killed him with their clubs. Then they took back the golden chain.

The crow and his wife lived in peace.

Moral: A clever plan can beat a strong enemy.
