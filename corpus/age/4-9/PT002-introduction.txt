Introduction

The story of the Panchatantra

In a city called Maidens' Delight, there lived a king named Immortal-Power. He was a wise king with many friends. His three sons were Rich-Power, Fierce-Power, and Endless-Power. They were very silly.

The king was not happy with his sons. They did not like to learn. The king called his advisors and said, "My sons are not smart. They do not like to learn. I am very sad."

The advisors said, "Learning takes a long time. It takes twelve years to learn grammar. Then you can learn about life and the world." One advisor, Keen, said, "Learning is hard. It takes too long. We need a way to make them smart fast."

Keen said, "There is a wise man named Vishnusharman. He is very smart. He can make my sons smart quickly." The king called Vishnusharman and said, "Please make my sons smart. I will give you a hundred land-grants."

Vishnusharman said, "I am not a seller of knowledge. I will make your sons smart in six months or I will give up my name." The king was surprised and happy.

Vishnusharman took the boys home and made them learn five books. The books were about friends, winning friends, and being smart. The boys learned the books in six months. They became very smart and the king was happy.

Moral: Learning is very important for a happy life.
