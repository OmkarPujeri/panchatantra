Gold's Gloom

There was a hermit named Crop-Ear.
He lived in a cell near a shrine.
He would beg for food in the city.
He would eat and then hide the leftovers.

A mouse would come and eat the food.
Crop-Ear would move the bowl to a higher place.
The mouse would still find the food and eat it.

One day, a guest named Wide-Bottom came.
Crop-Ear welcomed him and gave him food.
They talked and laughed together at night.

But Crop-Ear's mind was on the mouse.
He kept hitting the bowl with a stick.
Wide-Bottom got angry and left the cell.

Wide-Bottom said, "You don't care about me.
You only care about your food and your cell."

Crop-Ear said, "No, I care about you.
But there's a mouse that eats my food.
I hit the bowl to scare it away."

Wide-Bottom said, "But have you found the mouse-hole?"
Crop-Ear said, "No, I haven't."

Wide-Bottom said, "The mouse-hole must be near its food.
The smell of its hoard makes it strong."

Crop-Ear said, "How did you know that?"
Wide-Bottom said, "Because I've lived a long time."

Crop-Ear was happy to have Wide-Bottom as a friend.
He learned that kindness and friendship are important.

Moral: A kind heart and a listening ear make good friends.
