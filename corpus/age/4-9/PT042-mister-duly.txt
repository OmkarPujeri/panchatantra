Mister Duly

There was a merchant named Ocean. He had a son.

The son bought a book for a hundred rupees. The book had one line: "What's duly his, a man receives."

Ocean saw the book and asked his son, "What did you pay for this book?" The son said, "A hundred rupees." Ocean said, "You fool! How can you make money with one line in the book?" He kicked his son out of the house.

The son went to another country. He met people and said, "What's duly his, a man receives." The people called him Mister Duly.

One day, a princess named Moonlight saw a handsome prince. She fell in love with him. She sent her friend to ask the prince to meet her. The prince agreed to meet her.

But the prince thought, "Is it right to go to the princess's house at night?" He read a book and found these lines: "A Brahman-slayer, so they say, / Is he who tries to house / With teacher's child, or wife of friend, / Or royal servant's spouse." He decided not to go.

Mister Duly saw a strap hanging from a house. He climbed up the strap to see what was inside. The princess thought the prince was the one she had sent for. She gave him food, clothes, and a bath. But when the prince spoke, she realized he was not the one she loved. She sent him away.

Mister Duly went to a temple and slept. A policeman came to the temple. Mister Duly said, "What's duly his, a man receives." The policeman said, "This temple is empty. Sleep in my bed."

But Mister Duly lay in the wrong bed. The policeman's daughter, Naughty, woke up and thought Mister Duly was her boyfriend. She married him in the dark. But when she saw him, she scolded him and sent him away.

Mister Duly joined a wedding procession. An elephant came and scared everyone. Mister Duly saved the bride and chased the elephant away.

The king came and asked Mister Duly, "What happened?" Mister Duly said, "What's duly his, a man receives." The princess, the policeman's daughter, and the merchant's daughter told their tales. The king heard them all. He learned the truth.

The king gave Mister Duly his daughter and a thousand villages. He made Mister Duly his crown prince. Mister Duly and his family lived happily ever after.

Moral: What's duly yours, you will receive.
