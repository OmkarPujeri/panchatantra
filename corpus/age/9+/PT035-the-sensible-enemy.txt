The Sensible Enemy

In a kingdom, a prince befriended a merchant's son and the son of a man of learning. They spent their days together, enjoying various diversions and pastimes.

The prince often complained to his friends about his father's disapproval of his lack of interest in kingly pursuits like archery and elephant-riding. His friends sympathized with him and said, "We too face similar troubles at home. But now that we see you're also unhappy, we're even more grieved."

The prince decided it was time to leave and find a new life elsewhere. He suggested traveling to Climbing Mountain, where they could find precious gems and satisfy their desires.

The merchant's son agreed, saying, "We need money to achieve our goals. Climbing Mountain is the perfect place to find gems." They set off together.

On Climbing Mountain, each of them discovered a magnificent gem. However, they soon realized they needed to protect their treasures from thieves. The son of the man of learning proposed an unusual plan: they would swallow the gems and carry them in their stomachs.

As they followed this plan, a fellow traveler, who had been watching them, devised a scheme to steal the gems. He joined their group, claiming he needed their protection to reach his home.

The travelers continued their journey, unaware of the danger lurking among them. As they passed through a Bhil village, a bird in a cage began to sing. The village chief, understanding the bird's song, realized they were carrying precious gems.

The chief sent his men to capture the travelers, but they found nothing. The bird sang again, and the chief, convinced of the gems' existence, had the travelers brought back for further inspection.

The chief threatened to cut open their stomachs to find the gems, but the thief had a plan. He knew that if his own stomach was cut open first and nothing was found, the chief would spare the others.

The thief humbly asked the chief to cut his stomach first, and the chief, moved by his words, agreed. However, when the chief examined the thief's stomach, he found no gem.

Realizing his mistake, the chief set the three friends free. The thief had died to save them. They hurried through the forest and reached a safe place.

Moral: A sensible enemy can be a better friend than a selfish ally.
