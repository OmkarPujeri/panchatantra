The Brahman's Goat

In a small town, a Brahman named Friendly tended the sacred fire. One rainy day, he went to another village to find a sacrifice for the new moon. He met a man who gave him a plump goat, as the scriptures prescribed.

Friendly put the goat through its paces, found it healthy, and started back home. On the road, he met three hungry rogues who eyed the goat on his shoulder. They whispered to each other, "Let's trick him, get the goat, and warm up on this cold day."

The first rogue changed his clothes and approached Friendly, saying, "Brahman, what are you doing? You're carrying a dog on your shoulder! Don't you know the verse about dogs and other unclean animals?" Friendly got angry and said, "You're blind! That's a goat, not a dog!"

The rogue apologized and said, "Don't be angry, sir. I spoke in ignorance." But when Friendly continued on his way, the second rogue met him and said, "Sir, that's a calf you're carrying! You shouldn't put a lifeless animal on your shoulder." Friendly got angry again and said, "You're blind! That's a goat, not a calf!"

The rogue apologized, but when Friendly walked a little farther, the third rogue met him and said, "Sir, you're carrying a donkey on your shoulder! You should wash your clothes and bathe if you touch one." Friendly got terrified and threw the goat on the ground, thinking it was a goblin.

The three rogues caught the goat and carried out their plan. Friendly returned home, feeling foolish. He realized that the rogues had tricked him with their clever words.

Moral: Cunning words can deceive even the wisest, and it's easy to be tricked by clever lies.
