Loss of Gains

On the shore of the sea stood a great rose-apple tree that was never without fruit. In its branches lived a monkey named Red-Face. One day a crocodile named Ugly-Mug crawled out of the ocean and burrowed in the soft sand beneath the tree.

"You are my guest," said Red-Face. "Please eat these rose-apples, for they taste like nectar." He threw down the fruit, and the crocodile ate it happily. The two became fast friends, and every day the crocodile came to rest in the shade of the tree while they talked for hours about all kinds of things.

The crocodile carried the leftover fruit home to his wife. "Where do you find such sweet fruit?" she asked. "My good friend the monkey gives it to me," he said. Then she said, "If anyone eats such nectar every day, his heart must be full of nectar too. If you love me, bring me his heart to eat, and then I will never grow old or sick." The crocodile refused. "He is my friend, and he gives us fruit. I cannot kill him." But his wife grew angry with him. "You love that monkey more than me," she said. "Unless I eat his heart, I will starve myself to death." She would not eat at all, so at last the crocodile gave in.

He went to the tree looking very sad. "My friend," said the monkey, "why are you so gloomy, and why have you stayed away so long?" The crocodile lied to him. "My wife scolded me," he said. "She said I never repay your kindness. Come home with us, so that we can thank you properly. Climb on my back, and I will carry you across the water." Red-Face trusted his friend and climbed onto the crocodile's back.

But as they swam out into the deep sea, the monkey grew frightened. "Go slowly, brother," he said. "The big waves are soaking me." Then the crocodile thought, "He is far from shore now, so he is in my power." So he said, "My friend, I have tricked you, for my wife wants to eat your heart. Pray to your favorite god." Red-Face was terrified, yet his quick mind did not fail him. "Oh no!" he cried. "Why did you not tell me on shore? Then I would have brought my other heart. I keep it in a hole in the rose-apple tree." When the crocodile heard this, he was delighted. "Then give me that other heart," he said, "and my wife will stop starving herself. I will take you back to the tree."

He turned and swam back toward the shore. When they reached the sand, the monkey leaped off and scrambled up the tree. "Hurrah!" he thought. "My life is saved." The crocodile called up, "My friend, give me the heart." Then Red-Face laughed and scolded him. "You fool! You traitor! Who ever has two hearts? Go home, and never come back under this tree."

The crocodile felt ashamed of himself, and he wondered why he had been such a fool as to tell the monkey his plan. He tried to win back the monkey's trust. "It was only a joke," he said. "Come to our house as a guest." But the monkey would not listen. "Rascal, go away," he said. "I will not come."

Later a water-beast brought him news that his wife had starved herself to death, and the crocodile was broken-hearted. But the monkey only laughed at him. "I knew from the start that you were henpecked," he said. Then a second messenger arrived: while the crocodile was away, a big strange crocodile had seized his home. Now the crocodile was truly miserable, for his wife was dead, his friend was angry, and a stranger sat in his house. He turned to the monkey for advice. "Tell me what to do," he begged. "Shall I fight him, or speak to him softly?" At first the monkey refused to help. "You ungrateful wretch," he said, "you are a fool, so I will give you no advice." But at last, moved by his old friend's trouble, Red-Face spoke. "Be brave," he said. "Fight for what is yours, and do not give up your home."

So the crocodile said farewell to the monkey and went to his house. He joined battle with the ruffian who had forced a way in. He fought with all his courage, killed the intruder, and won back his home. He lived there happily for a long time.

Moral: A quick mind can escape a trap, and courage can win back what folly has lost.
