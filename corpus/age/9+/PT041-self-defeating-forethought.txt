Self-defeating Forethought

In a small village, a hillman named Karna loved to hunt. He went to the forest, eager to hunt and increase his sins.

As he walked, he came across a large boar with sharp tusks. Karna quickly drew his bow and arrow, ready to shoot. He thought, "I must kill this boar to prove my bravery." He took aim and shot the boar with a sharp arrow.

The boar, enraged, charged at Karna and bit his stomach with its sharp fangs. Karna fell to the ground, dead. The boar, wounded by the arrow, also died soon after.

A starving jackal, named Loka, stumbled upon the scene. He saw the dead boar and Karna and thought, "What a wonderful discovery! Fate has provided me with a feast." Loka thought, "My ancestors were wise when they said, 'The fruit of our actions, good or bad, comes back to us.'"

Loka decided to eat the sinew wrapped around the bow-tip, thinking it would give him food for many days. He carefully took the sinew into his mouth and began to eat slowly. But, as he ate, the bow-tip snapped and pierced the roof of his mouth.

Loka's pain was intense, and he died soon after. The villagers found both the hillman and the jackal dead, and they said, "Karna and Loka both met their deaths through their own foolishness."

Moral: Indulge in no excessive greed, as it can lead to your own downfall.
