Ill-considered Action

In the city of Trumpet-Flower, a merchant named Jewel lived a life of virtue, but his fortune was lost due to fate's decree. He became despondent and thought, "Poverty is a curse! After money disappears, one loses all worth."

He decided to abandon life by self-starvation, but a Jain monk appeared in his dream, claiming to be a trillion dollars earned by his ancestors. The monk instructed Jewel to club him on the head, and he would turn to gold.

On waking, Jewel pondered the dream, thinking it would prove false. A barber arrived, and while he was busy, the monk appeared. Jewel, delighted, struck the monk with a stick, turning him to gold.

The barber, seeing the monk's transformation, thought, "I'll do the same!" He invited many monks to his house and clubbed them. Some died, and others had their heads broken and cried out in pain. However, the noise alerted the soldiers, who discovered the barber's cruel deed.

The barber was taken to court, where he confessed his actions, blaming Jewel for advising him. The judges summoned Jewel and questioned him about the Jain monk. Jewel explained the original monk's appearance, and the judges condemned the barber to be impaled.

Moral: Take warning from the barber: a deed done without thinking brings trouble on the one who does it.
