Mouse-Maid Made Mouse

The Ganges River was dotted with foamy waves created by frightened fish leaping from the water. On its banks stood a hermitage, home to many holy men who practiced self-denial and devotion. They wore loincloths made of bark and lived on a diet of fruits, roots, and moss.

Yajnavalkya, the leader of the hermitage, was a wise and holy man. One day, a little female mouse fell from a hawk's beak into his hand. He bathed and purified himself, then used his magic to transform the mouse into a girl.

Yajnavalkya's wife had been childless, and she was overjoyed to have a new daughter. She pampered the girl and raised her as her own. As the girl grew older, Yajnavalkya's wife suggested that it was time for her to get married. Yajnavalkya agreed, saying that the time had come for her to be married.

The holy man wanted to marry his daughter to the best possible partner. He decided to marry her to the sun, the cloud, the wind, or the mountain, whichever one he thought was the most suitable. He summoned the sun first, but his daughter rejected him, saying he was too hot.

Next, Yajnavalkya summoned the cloud, but his daughter was not impressed with its black and frigid appearance. She asked him to find someone even better. The holy man then summoned the wind, but his daughter found it too fidgety. She asked him to find someone even more superior.

Finally, the holy man asked the mountain if it was superior to the wind, but the mountain replied that the mouse was even more superior. Yajnavalkya summoned a mouse, and his daughter was overjoyed. She asked him to turn her into a mouse, so she could be with her kind.

Yajnavalkya used his magic to transform his daughter into a mouse, and she was married to the mouse. From that day on, she lived happily as a mouse, doing mouse things.

Moral: A person's true nature and happiness can be found when they are with those of their own kind.
