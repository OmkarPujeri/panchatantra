Forethought, Readywit, and Fatalist

In a great lake ringed with reeds, three full-grown fish had lived all their lives. Their names were Forethought, Readywit, and Fatalist. The water was deep and still, and the reeds gave good cover.

One day, as Forethought drifted near the bank, he overheard some passers-by talking with fishermen. "There are plenty of fish in this pond," they were saying to each other. "Tomorrow we will come with our nets and fish it."

Forethought listened, and his heart sank. He thought, "This is bad news. Tomorrow, or the day after, they are sure to come. I will take Readywit and Fatalist to another lake, one whose waters are never troubled. We cannot stay here." So he called his two friends and put the question to them.

Readywit heard him out, but he was not troubled. "I have lived in this lake a long time," he said, "and I cannot move out of it in such a hurry. If the fishermen come, I will think of some way to save myself. I will find it when the moment is upon me."

Fatalist was even more certain. "There are plenty of big lakes elsewhere," he said. "Who knows whether they will come here at all? A fish should not leave the lake of his birth for a bit of small gossip. I am determined not to go."

When Forethought saw that both their minds were made up, he did not argue with them. He had warned them of the danger, and they would not listen. So he said goodbye to his friends, and he swam away alone. He went down the channel into another body of water. There no fisherman ever disturbed the peace.

The next day the fishermen came, exactly as Forethought had feared. They came with their boys and their nets, and they surrounded the little pool at the heart of the lake. Then they cast their net into the water. When they drew it in, they had caught every fish in the pond. Not a single one slipped free.

Readywit was still in the water when the net closed. He lay perfectly still. He let his body float as if life had already left it. The fishermen hauled the net onto the bank and looked at his great, quiet shape. "This big fellow died without any help," they said. So they pulled him from the net and laid him on the grass. The instant their eyes turned away, Readywit twisted his body. He wriggled back into the water and was safe.

But Fatalist had no such plan. When the net gathered around him, he thrust his nose into the meshes. He struggled with all his strength, but the more he fought, the more tightly the cords held him. The fishermen saw the net thrashing. They beat him again and again with their heavy clubs, until at last he lay still. And so poor Fatalist died upon the shore. It was the lake he would not leave.

Moral: Think ahead and be prepared, or you might not survive.
