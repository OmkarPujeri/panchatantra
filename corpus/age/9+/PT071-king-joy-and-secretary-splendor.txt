King Joy and Secretary Splendor

The kingdom of King Joy was renowned for its grandeur, with princes from far and wide paying homage to their ruler.

King Joy's secretary, Splendor, was a wise and learned man, but his wife often found fault with him. One day, she pouted and refused to speak to him.

Splendor begged his wife to tell him what was wrong and promised to fix it. His wife finally relented, saying that he must shave his head and fall at her feet to make amends.

Splendor did as he was told, and his wife forgave him. But King Joy's wife was not so easily placated. She refused to forgive him, no matter how many apologies he made.

In desperation, King Joy did something silly. He held a bridle in his mouth and let his wife ride on his back, making horse-like noises as he was led around the palace.

The next morning, Splendor appeared before the king, looking rather silly with his shaved head. The king asked him why he was in such a state, and Splendor explained that he had been forced to do it by his wife.

The king was amused and said, "It seems that you, too, are under your wife's thumb, just like me. Your own words betrayed you."

"Remember," the king said, "the parrots and the grackles are caged because they speak. The silent herons go free. Silence is a master-key."

Moral: Silence is often the best policy, especially when dealing with someone who is being unreasonable.
