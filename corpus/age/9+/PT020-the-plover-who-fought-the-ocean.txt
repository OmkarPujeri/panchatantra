The Plover Who Fought the Ocean

 

Sprawl and his wife Constance lived by the sea, where fish, crocodiles, and turtles thrived. Constance was pregnant and needed a safe place to lay her eggs. "Find a spot for me," she said to Sprawl. "Why worry?" he replied, "our home is perfect. The ocean can't harm us."

But Constance wasn't convinced. "What if the ocean's tide reaches us and swallows our babies?" she asked. Sprawl chuckled and began to boast about his bravery. "I'm not afraid of the ocean! Who dares to challenge me?" he said. But his wife wasn't impressed.

Constance laughed at Sprawl's boasts and said, "You're just a show-off! You think you're brave, but you don't know your own strength and weakness. It's hard to know yourself, but it's essential to success." She reminded him of a wise saying: "Accurate self-critics are secure in times of trouble."

Constance laid her eggs, but the ocean had overheard all the boasting. One day, while the plovers were away, he reached out with his wave-hands and seized the eggs. Constance was heartbroken and blamed her husband's pride. Sprawl vowed to dry up the ocean with his bill. Constance urged him to summon the other birds for help. The birds came, and they beat the water with their wings and tried to fill it with clods of dust, but the ocean was far too vast. Then an old gander advised them to seek Garuda, the king of birds. Garuda served the god Vishnu, and he was angry that the ocean had wronged his servant. Vishnu himself came and reproached the ocean. He fitted a fiery arrow to his bow and ordered the ocean to return the eggs. Trembling with fear, the ocean restored the eggs to the plover.

Moral: Know your own strength and take the advice of your friends.
