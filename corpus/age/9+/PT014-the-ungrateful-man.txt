The Ungrateful Man

In a small town, a Brahman named Sacrifice lived with his wife and children. His wife constantly scolded him, saying, "You're so lazy! Get out and find food for us!" Weary of her complaints, the Brahman set out on a long journey.

While wandering through a dense forest, he stumbled upon a well overgrown with grass. Peering inside, he saw a tiger, a monkey, a snake, and a man trapped at the bottom. They also saw him.

The tiger spoke up, "Please save me, noble Brahman. Think of the good deed you'll do." The Brahman hesitated, "I'm afraid of you. Your name alone sends shivers down my spine." The tiger replied, "I swear to you on three oaths that no harm will come to you. Have pity and pull me out."

The Brahman thought, "If disaster comes, it's a disaster that leads to salvation." He pulled the tiger out. Next, the monkey and snake asked for help, and the Brahman obliged.

The animals warned the Brahman, "Beware of the man trapped down there. He's a sinner, and you shouldn't trust him." But the Brahman, feeling sorry for the man, pulled him out too.

The man introduced himself as a goldsmith and asked the Brahman to bring him gold to work on. The Brahman visited the goldsmith, who welcomed him warmly, but then revealed that he had worked on the gold that the prince had lost.

The goldsmith took the gold to the king, who recognized it as the stolen treasure from his son. The king ordered the Brahman to be arrested and impaled.

The snake appeared and promised to bite the king's queen, poisoning her, but only the Brahman's touch could cure her. The Brahman was freed from his fetters and cured the queen by touching her.

The king apologized to the Brahman and asked him to reveal the truth about the gold. The Brahman told the whole story, and the king arrested the goldsmith, giving the Brahman a thousand villages and appointing him privy counselor.

The Brahman returned home, where he was welcomed by his family and friends. He lived happily, performing numerous sacrifices and attending to his royal duties.

Moral: Gratitude is the greatest virtue, and ungratefulness can lead to great suffering.
