The Lion-Makers

Four friends, Brahmans all, lived together in a small town. Three of them were very learned, but a bit foolish. One of them, however, was wise beyond his years.

The four friends met to discuss their future. "We've studied hard," they said, "but what's the point if we don't travel, gain the favor of kings, and make some money?" They decided to set out on a journey together.

But as they walked, the eldest of the group said, "One of us is holding us back. He's wise, but lacks the knowledge we've gained. We won't share our earnings with him. He should go back home."

The second friend agreed, saying, "You're right. He's not learned, and that's a problem. He should go home." But the third friend disagreed, "No, we've been friends since childhood. He'll share in our earnings."

The friends continued their journey, and soon came across the skeleton of a lion in the forest. "What a great opportunity to test our knowledge!" they exclaimed. "Let's bring this lion back to life using our scholarship."

The first friend assembled the skeleton, the second provided skin, flesh, and blood, and the third attempted to give the lion life. But the wise friend cautioned, "Don't do it! This lion will kill us all if you bring it back to life."

The others laughed at him, saying, "You're just a simpleton. We know what we're doing." But the wise friend replied, "Wait, I'll just climb this tree to get a better view."

While the others were distracted, the wise friend climbed the tree and watched as the lion came to life. The lion then rose up and killed the other three friends. But the wise friend climbed down and went home, safe and sound.

Moral: True wisdom and common sense are more valuable than all the knowledge in the world.
