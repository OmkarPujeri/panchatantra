The Farmer's Wife

In a small village, a farmer lived with his elderly wife. She was unhappy, always thinking of other men.

A cunning rogue noticed her and proposed a plan. He said his wife was dead, and he was smitten with love for her. He asked her to bring her husband's money, so they could leave together and enjoy life.

The farmer's wife agreed, and that night, she took all the money from their home. The next morning, she met the rogue, and he promised to cross the river with her on his back. But when they reached the river, he took the money and her clothes, leaving her alone on the riverbank.

As she sat crying, a she-jackal appeared, carrying a piece of meat. A fish jumped out of the water, and a vulture swooped down, taking the meat. The jackal chased after the fish, but it escaped into the river.

The farmer's wife smiled at the jackal's misfortune and said, "You're just like me – lost and alone." The jackal replied, "At least I have my hunger to distract me. You're left with nothing."

Just then, a crocodile appeared and began to tell a story about a farmer's wife who had been tricked by a rogue. The story was interrupted by a second crocodile, who reported that his house had been occupied by another crocodile.

The first crocodile was worried and asked his monkey friend for advice. At first the monkey refused to help. He said the crocodile was a fool, and told a story about a foolish monkey who broke a sparrow's nest. But the crocodile begged his old friend again and again. Then the monkey told him to fight the intruder who had taken his house. The crocodile fought the intruder and killed him. So he got his home back and lived there happily.

Moral: Misfortunes often come in threes, and it's essential to be prepared to face them wisely.
