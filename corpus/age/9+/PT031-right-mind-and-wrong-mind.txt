Right-Mind and Wrong-Mind

In a bustling city, two friends, Right-Mind and Wrong-Mind, lived with their merchant fathers. They decided to travel to a distant land to earn money and make their families proud.

As they wandered, Right-Mind stumbled upon a hidden pot containing a thousand dinars, left behind by a wise holy man. He shared the news with Wrong-Mind, and they agreed to return home together.

Upon their return, Right-Mind suggested they split the treasure equally, but Wrong-Mind proposed a clever plan. He convinced Right-Mind to divide the money into smaller portions, burying the rest and promising to share the remaining wealth.

Right-Mind, trusting his friend, agreed to the plan. Wrong-Mind, however, had no intention of sharing. He spent his share recklessly, and soon he needed more. He convinced Right-Mind to divide the remaining treasure, and eventually, he convinced himself that it was okay to steal the rest.

When Right-Mind discovered the theft, Wrong-Mind accused him of stealing the treasure. The two friends took their dispute to court, where magistrates decided to conduct an ordeal to determine the guilty party.

Wrong-Mind claimed he had a witness, the goddess of the forest. That night, he hid his father in a hole in the mimosa tree near the buried treasure. In the morning, the magistrates gathered at the tree, and Wrong-Mind called out to the goddess. A voice from the tree said, "Right-Mind took the money." The magistrates believed it and prepared to punish Right-Mind. But Right-Mind piled dry branches around the tree and set it on fire. Wrong-Mind's father burst out of the hole, scorched and sobbing, and confessed that it was all Wrong-Mind's doing. The king's men hanged Wrong-Mind on a branch of the mimosa tree, and honoured Right-Mind for his innocence.

Moral: Clever plans often hide a hidden trap, and trusting someone who is not trustworthy can lead to trouble.
