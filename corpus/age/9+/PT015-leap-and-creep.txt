Leap and Creep

In a grand palace, a majestic bed stood, famous for its comfort and beauty. A plump and handsome louse named Creep lived on the bed's coverlet with her large family. She fed on the king's blood while he slept, growing stronger and healthier.

One day, a flea named Leap drifted in on the wind and landed on the bed. He was thrilled to see the luxurious coverlet, soft pillows, and sweet perfume. Leap was charmed by the bed's delightfulness and began to explore, eventually meeting Creep. She scolded him, "Where do you come from? This is a royal bed. Leave now and don't waste any more time."

Leap replied, "Madam, you shouldn't speak like that. As a guest, I deserve respect. I've tasted blood from various people, but this king's blood must be special. It's like nectar, free from impurities. I'd love to taste it and enjoy the pleasure and benefit."

Creep was taken aback by Leap's words. She remembered the story of a cunning prince who quoted a proverb to show respect to his guest. She agreed to let Leap stay, but with a condition: "When the king is drunk, tired, or asleep, then you may quietly bite him on the feet. That is the right time and place."

However, Leap misunderstood the condition and bit the king on the back when he was dozing off. The king was shocked and woke up, scratching his back and shouting for his servants to find the insect. Leap panicked and hid in a crevice, but the servants found Creep and her family, killing them instead.

The servants searched the bed, but Leap was nowhere to be found. They left, and the king was left with a painful scratch.

Moral: Do not share your house with a stranger. Welcoming the flea Leap is what brought death to Creep the louse.
