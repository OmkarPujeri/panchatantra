The Girl Who Married a Snake

In a kingdom called Palace City, a Brahman named Godly lived with his wife. She was childless and often wept at the sight of her neighbors' children.

One day, Godly said to his wife, "Don't be sad. Remember when I offered a sacrifice for a child? An invisible voice said I'd have a son who would surpass everyone in beauty, character, and charm."

The wife's heart swelled with joy. "I hope these promises come true," she said. Soon, she gave birth to a snake. Her friends advised her to throw it away, but she took care of it like a baby.

She bathed the snake, fed it milk and butter, and kept it in a clean box. Before long, the snake grew up. The Brahman's wife was so proud of her son.

At a neighbor's son's wedding, the Brahman's wife said to her husband, "Why don't you arrange a wedding for our son?" Godly replied, "Who would give their daughter to a snake? I'd have to go to the underworld and beg Vasuki, the serpent-king."

Godly was disturbed by his wife's sad face, so he packed his bags and traveled to a distant land called Kutkuta City. He stayed with a kinsman who treated him kindly.

The next morning, Godly's host asked, "What brought you here?" Godly replied, "I'm searching for a bride for my son." His host said, "I have a beautiful daughter. Take her for your son."

The Brahman took the girl and her attendants back home. When the people saw her, they were amazed by her beauty. They said, "How can we give such a wonderful girl to a snake?" The girl's relatives were worried, but she said, "I'll marry the snake, and that's final."

So she married the snake. One night, the serpent left the chest and climbed into her bed. She saw a man and jumped up in fear. But he said, "Stay, my dear wife. I am your husband." To prove it, he slipped back into the snake's body. Then he came out again as a man. He shone with a diadem, earrings, and rings. She fell at his feet, and they embraced with joy. Early next morning, Godly found the snake skin. He burned it, saying, "I do not want him to enter that again." Then he and his wife showed everyone their handsome son.

Moral: True promises and fate are unchangeable, and we must accept what is destined.
