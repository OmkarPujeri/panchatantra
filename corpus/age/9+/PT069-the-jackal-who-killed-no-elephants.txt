The Jackal Who Killed No Elephants

In a forest, a lion and his wife lived with their twins. The lion hunted every day, bringing back deer and other animals for his wife to eat. One day, the lion came back empty-handed, but found a baby jackal on the trail.

He picked up the jackal cub and brought it home, giving it to his wife. She asked if he had brought any food, and he said, "No, but I found this jackal cub. I didn't kill it because it's a baby like our twins." He told her a story about being kind to those who are weak.

The lioness was moved by his words and decided to raise the jackal cub as her own. She fed it her own milk and made it very fat. The three cubs played together, unaware of their different parentage.

One day, a wild elephant wandered into the forest. The lion twins saw it and wanted to attack, but the jackal cub warned them, "Don't go near him, brothers. He's an enemy of your kind." The lion twins were frightened and retreated.

When they told their parents about the elephant, the jackal cub heard them and was angry. He scolded his brothers, saying they should respect him as their equal. But the lioness took him aside and said, "You must never speak to your brothers like that. They are your family."

The jackal cub's anger grew, and he threatened to kill his brothers. The lioness calmly told him the truth: "You're a jackal, and you were raised by me because I pitied you. But if your brothers find out, they'll fight you. Go join your own people before it's too late."

The jackal cub was terrified and slunk away to join his own family. He realized he didn't belong with the lions.

Moral: Being raised by someone else doesn't change your true nature.
