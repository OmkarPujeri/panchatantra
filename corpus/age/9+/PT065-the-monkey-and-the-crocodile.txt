The Monkey and the Crocodile

A great rose-apple tree stood on the shore of the sea, its branches laden with fruit. Red-Face, a mischievous monkey, lived in the tree. One day, a crocodile named Ugly-Mug crawled out of the ocean and burrowed into the soft sand.

Red-Face welcomed the crocodile, offering him rose-apples and saying, "You're my guest, sir. Eat these delicious fruits, and we'll have a lovely conversation." The crocodile enjoyed the rose-apples and the conversation that followed.

When the crocodile returned home, he gave his wife the uneaten rose-apples. She asked him, "Where do you get these nectar fruits?" The crocodile replied, "I have a good friend, a monkey named Red-Face, who gives them to me."

His wife's eyes lit up with desire, and she said, "If you eat these fruits every day, your heart must be turned to nectar. Give me your friend's heart, and I'll eat it. Then I'll never grow old or sick, and I'll be a delightful companion for you."

The crocodile refused, explaining that Red-Face was like a brother to him. He couldn't kill him, especially since he gave them fruit. The crocodile's wife wouldn't give up, accusing him of loving Red-Face and spending all his time with her.

The crocodile was dejected, but he tried to reason with his wife. He said, "When I'm at your feet, why do you look at me with jealousy?" His wife's face swelled with tears, and she scolded him, saying, "You love Red-Face, don't you? His tricks have stolen your heart. My rivalry is futile, sir. Please abstain from this tricky game."

The crocodile visited Red-Face. Red-Face was amused and said, "My friend, why have you not been here for so long? Why don't you speak cheerfully and tell a witty joke?" The crocodile said his wife had scolded him for not returning Red-Face's kindness. She wanted Red-Face to come home as a guest.

Red-Face suggested that the crocodile's wife come to the rose-apple tree, but the crocodile said, "My home is on a lovely sand-bank under the water. Climb on my back, and we'll travel comfortably." Red-Face agreed, but as they swam, he became terrified, thinking, "If I fall from his back, I'll drown."

The crocodile thought, "If he falls, he's doomed. I'll tell him my plan, and he can pray to his favorite god." He said, "Sir, I've deceived you, and you'll die because my wife ordered me to do it. Pray to your favorite god." Red-Face asked, "Why did you plan to kill me? What harm have I done your wife or you?"

The crocodile explained that his wife wanted to eat Red-Face's heart because she longed to taste the nectar fruits. Red-Face replied, "If that's the case, why didn't you tell me on shore? I have another heart, very sweet indeed, which I keep in a hole in the rose-apple tree."

The crocodile was delighted and said, "Give me that other heart, and my cross wife will eat it and give up starving herself. I'll take you back to the rose-apple tree." Red-Face laughed and scolded the crocodile, saying, "You fool! You traitor! How can anyone have two hearts? Go home, and never come back under the rose-apple tree."

The crocodile was embarrassed, thinking, "Oh, why was I such a fool as to tell him my plan? If I can win his confidence again, I'll do it." He said, "My friend, she has no need of a heart. What I said was just a joke to test your sentiments. Please come to our house as a guest. Your brother's wife is most eager for you."

Red-Face refused, saying, "Rascal! Go away this moment. I will not come. Tell Handsome, miss, that Theodore will see him in the well no more."

Moral: Those who trust a faithless friend are certain to fall into danger.
