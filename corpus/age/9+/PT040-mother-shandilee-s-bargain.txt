Mother Shandilee's Bargain

 

A Brahman named Shandilee came to live with a family in a town for the rainy season. He helped with household chores and did his daily prayers.

One morning, Shandilee overheard his host and his wife talking about the winter solstice. The Brahman said, "My dear, it's a great time for donations. We should feed a poor Brahman." But his wife snapped back, "Who would give to a poor Brahman like you? You're not even ashamed?"

Shandilee's wife was bitter about their poverty. She said, "I've never had a thing since I married you. We've never eaten fancy food or even seen a gem or a ring." The Brahman was taken aback and stuttered, "My dear, you shouldn't say that. Even the poor should give what they can to those in need."

Shandilee reminded his wife about the importance of giving. "Even a small gift can make a big difference. The cloud gives only water, but the whole world treats it with respect. We should give what we can, when we can." He also quoted a wise saying, "A little helps in time of need, but excessive greed leads to trouble."

At last his wife agreed. "I have a little sesame at home," she said. "I will grind it into flour and feed a Brahman." Her husband was pleased. He set off for another village.

Then the wife softened the sesame in hot water and took off the hulls. She laid the grain out in the sun to dry. But while she was busy in the house, a dog made water on the dish of grain. "See how fate turns against me," she thought. "Even this grain is now unfit to eat. But it still looks clean. I will take it to a neighbor. I will trade my hulled grain for her unhulled kind. Anyone would agree to such a bargain." So she carried her basket from house to house. She called, "Who will trade unhulled sesame for my hulled sesame?"

A neighbor's wife was delighted. She made the trade at once. When her husband came home, he asked, "My dear, what is this?" She told him, "I traded unhulled sesame for hulled." He pondered a moment. Then he asked, "Whose grain was this?" His son answered, "It belonged to the Brahman's wife." Then he said, "My dear, she is very shrewd at a bargain. You had better throw this sesame away."

Moral: A shrewd bargain can look like a gift, but the other person pays the price.
