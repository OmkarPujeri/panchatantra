How the Birds Picked a King

The birds of different species gathered for a consultation.

There were swans, cranes, nightingales, peacocks, plovers, owls, doves, pigeons, partridges, bluejays, vultures, skylarks, demoiselles, cuckoos, and woodpeckers. They complained about their current king, Garuda, who ignored their problems and didn't defend them when needed.

The birds decided to choose a new king, and since the owl looked wise, they suggested him for the job. They prepared a grand ceremony, filling the air with the scent of holy streams, flowers, and incense.

As the owl waited for the anointing, a crow suddenly appeared, thinking, "What's all this fuss about?" The birds welcomed the crow, thinking he was the wisest of birds, and asked for his opinion.

The crow, however, laughed and said, "You're making a huge mistake! Why choose a blind owl when you have such beautiful and talented birds as yourselves? The owl is ugly, fierce, and mean-spirited. You'll fail with him as your king."

The crow reminded them that they already had a good king, Garuda, and that having two kings would be chaotic. He quoted proverbs to emphasize his point, saying that a single, strong king was better than multiple weak ones.

The birds looked at each other, then agreed with the crow. "He speaks well," they said, and flew home to think again about choosing a king.

Only the owl stayed behind, for he was blind in the daytime. He sat on his throne, still waiting to be anointed. "Who takes my orders?" he called. "Why is the ceremony delayed?"

His mate said, "The crow has found a way to stop the ceremony, and the birds have all flown away."

The owl was deeply disappointed. "You have wronged me by preventing my anointing," he said to the crow. "From this day there is enmity between us."

So the owl went home with his mate. And the crow, left alone, saw that he had made a needless enemy by speaking so harshly. Then he too left the spot.

Moral: Harsh words spoken without need can make an enemy for life.
