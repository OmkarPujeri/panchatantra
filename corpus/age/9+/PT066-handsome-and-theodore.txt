Handsome and Theodore

The Tale of Handsome and Theodore

In a well, a frog-king named Theodore lived with his relatives. They tormented him, so he thought of a plan to get rid of them. He remembered a proverb: "Pay both comfort and pain as they deserve." With this in mind, he invited a black snake named Handsome into the well.

Handsome was hesitant at first, thinking Theodore might be a conjurer or a druggist trying to trap him. But Theodore convinced him that he wanted to be friends. Handsome agreed, and Theodore showed him the hole where his relatives lived.

The snake ate them one by one, and when they were all gone, he asked Theodore for food. Theodore was worried, but he thought of another proverb: "A prudent soul gives a little to calm a fierce foe." So he decided to give Handsome one frog a day.

However, Theodore's wife warned him that this was a bad idea. She said, "If you give in to him, he'll eat everything in sight." But Theodore was determined to follow his plan. He gave Handsome a frog each day, and the snake ate them behind his back.

One day, Handsome ate Theodore's son, Theodosius. Theodore was devastated, but his wife told him to think of a plan to escape or kill the snake. Theodore left the well, and Handsome waited for his return. But when Theodore didn't come back, Handsome asked a lizard to find him and tell him to return quickly.

The lizard found Theodore and delivered the message, but Theodore refused to go back. He said, "A hungry man sticks to nothing, and the poor man has heartless tricks." He told the lizard to tell Handsome that he would never enter the well again.

Moral: When you give in to a fierce foe, you may lose everything, and it's hard to recover.
