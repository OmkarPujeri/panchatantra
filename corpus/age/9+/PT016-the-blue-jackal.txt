The Blue Jackal

Fierce-Howl, a hungry jackal, roamed the city streets at night, searching for food.

He stumbled into the house of a dyer, where he tumbled into a large indigo vat. The city dogs, who had been chasing him, gave up and went home.

Fierce-Howl managed to crawl out of the vat and escaped into the forest. The other animals were shocked by his blue body and fled in terror.

As they ran away, they said, "We don't know what this creature is or what it can do. It's not wise to trust something you don't understand." Fierce-Howl realized they were afraid of him and called out to them.

He said, "Don't be afraid! I'm your king, Fierce-Howl. Indra chose me to rule over the forest creatures because you didn't have a leader." The animals believed him and bowed down.

Fierce-Howl appointed the lion as prime minister and the tiger as lord of the bedchamber. He even made the elephant the doorkeeper and the monkey the bearer of the royal parasol. But he treated his fellow jackals poorly and chased them away.

The animals brought him food, which Fierce-Howl would divide and distribute to everyone. He enjoyed his new status as king.

One day, while he was sitting in his court, he heard a pack of jackals howling nearby. Fierce-Howl's body trembled with excitement, and he began to howl along with them. But when the other animals saw that he was a jackal, they were shocked and ashamed.

They said, "We've been deceived! Let's kill him!" Fierce-Howl tried to run away, but a tiger caught him and tore him apart.

Moral: Whoever betrays his true nature will eventually be discovered and punished.
