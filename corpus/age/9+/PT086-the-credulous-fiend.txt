The Credulous Fiend

In a far-off kingdom, King Fine-Army ruled with kindness and wisdom. His daughter, Princess Pearl, was known for her stunning beauty and charming smile. However, a wicked fiend had fallen in love with her and tried to carry her off every evening.

But the princess was clever and protected herself by drawing a magic circle. The fiend couldn't touch her, but he would often scare her with his evil presence. One evening, the princess confided in her friend about the fiend's nightly visits.

The fiend overheard their conversation and thought, "Ah, I'm not the only one after the princess! There's someone else, named Twilight, who also wants to take her away." He decided to disguise himself as a horse and investigate the palace stables to learn more about this rival.

Meanwhile, a horse-thief sneaked into the palace at night, looking for a valuable steed. He spotted the fiend-horse and thought it was the best horse in the stable. As he was about to leave, the fiend, still in horse form, was suddenly struck by the thief with a whip.

The fiend was terrified and started running, but the thief was confused. He thought, "This horse is acting strangely. It's not responding to the bit." The thief realized that the horse must be a fiend in disguise, and he decided to drop from a high spot to save his own life.

As the thief fell, he caught a branch from a banyan tree and stuck there. Both of them were relieved to have found a way to escape the other's clutches.

A mischievous monkey, a friend of the fiend, saw the situation and said, "Why are you running from a harmless man? Eat him!" The fiend transformed back into his true form, but his mind was still troubled. The thief, however, was now angry and took the monkey's tail in his mouth, causing the monkey immense pain.

The fiend, seeing the monkey's distress, tried to reason with him. He said, "You have been caught by Twilight, the one who wins the race." The monkey, still in agony, couldn't respond and could only wait for his fate to be decided.

Moral: Even the most cunning and powerful can fall prey to their own fears and doubts.
