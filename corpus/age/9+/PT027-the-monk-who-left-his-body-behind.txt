The Monk Who Left His Body Behind

The kingdom of Koshala had a mighty king named Fine-Chariot. He ruled over Unassailable, a city filled with princes who wore shining diadems.

A forest ranger came to report that the forest kings were in chaos, led by the fierce Vindhyaka. The king sent Counselor Strong to teach them discipline.

In the counselor's absence, a naked monk arrived in the city. He claimed to be an expert in astronomy and could read the stars. People were amazed by his knowledge and soon, the entire population looked up to him.

The king heard about the monk and summoned him to the palace. The monk was offered a seat and asked if he could read people's thoughts. He replied that it would be demonstrated later.

The king was curious and asked the monk to explain his abilities. The monk began to tell him about the secrets of the universe, and the king's curiosity grew.

One day, the monk failed to appear at the appointed hour. The next day, he entered the palace and announced that he had spent the previous day in heaven. He said he had gone there to ask the gods about the king's well-being.

The king was amazed and believed the monk's story. He became obsessed with the monk and neglected his royal duties. Counselor Strong returned from the forest and found the king ignoring his duties.

Strong was shocked to see the king's behavior and said, "Victory, O King! May the gods give you sense!" The king asked Strong about the monk and if he knew him. Strong replied that he had heard the monk was a master of his art and that he went to heaven every day.

The monk suggested that Strong could see for himself if he was in heaven. He went into his cell, locked the door, and waited. After a moment, Counselor Strong asked the king when the monk would return. The king replied that he would return with a new body.

Counselor Strong then asked for firewood to burn the monk's body. The king was puzzled, but Strong explained that he wanted to see if the monk would return with his heavenly body. The king agreed, and Strong had the monk's body burned. The monk never came back. He had been pretending all along.

Moral: A clever trickster can deceive even the most powerful people if they are blinded by curiosity and ignorance.
