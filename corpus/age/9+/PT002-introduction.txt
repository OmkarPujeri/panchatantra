Introduction

The Story of the King's Foolish Sons

In the southern country, there was a city called Maidens' Delight. King Immortal-Power lived there with his three sons, Rich-Power, Fierce-Power, and Endless-Power. However, these princes were complete blockheads.

The king noticed that his sons had no interest in learning. He called his advisors and said, "These sons of mine are not interested in education. They lack common sense. When I see them, my kingdom doesn't bring me happiness, no matter how many external problems I solve."

The advisors suggested different methods to educate the princes. They said, "Your Majesty, we can't teach them in a short time. They need to learn the basics of grammar, then move on to books on religion and practical life. It will take twelve years."

But one advisor, Keen, had a clever idea. He said, "Since life is short, and verbal sciences take a long time to master, let's create a summary of the essential facts. This way, we can make them intelligent in a short time."

The king liked Keen's idea and decided to find someone who could create this summary. He was introduced to a wise Brahman named Vishnusharman, who was known for his knowledge in many subjects.

Vishnusharman was asked to make the princes intelligent in six months' time. If he succeeded, the king would give him a hundred land-grants. But if he failed, he would have to give up his own name.

Vishnusharman agreed to take on the task. He said, "I'm not interested in money, and I'm not afraid of you, King. I'm eighty years old, and I've lost interest in worldly pleasures. But I'll show you my skills. If I fail, I'll give up my name."

The king was amazed by Vishnusharman's bold promise and entrusted the princes to him. Meanwhile, Vishnusharman took the princes home and made them learn five books he had written: "The Loss of Friends," "The Winning of Friends," "Crows and Owls," "Loss of Gains," and "Ill-considered Action."

The princes learned the books, and in six months' time, they became intelligent and knowledgeable. From that day on, the Panchatantra, or the "Five Books," traveled the world, teaching people how to live wisely.

Moral: The right guidance at the right time can change the course of a person's life and help them become intelligent and wise.
