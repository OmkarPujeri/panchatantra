The Old Man with the Young Wife

In a small town, a wealthy old merchant named Lovelorn lived. He had lost his wife, and his heart was still heavy with grief. To escape his loneliness, he married a young woman, the daughter of a poor shopkeeper.

The young woman, however, had no affection for the old man. She would often turn her back on him in bed and avoid his company. One night, while she was sleeping, a thief sneaked into the house. She was terrified and clung to the old man, thinking he was a protector.

The old man, surprised by her sudden affection, realized that she was hugging him out of fear of the thief. He smiled and said to the thief, "She usually shuns me, but now she's embracing me. Thank you for bringing us closer together. Take whatever you want."

The thief replied, "I want nothing here. But if I did want something, I would come back only when she does not cling to you like that." The old man realized that even a thief can be a benefactor if treated with kindness.

Foe-Crusher, the king, was advised by his counselors on how to deal with the situation. One counselor, Hook-Nose, suggested that the suppliant guest should not be killed. He explained, "We should expect help from our enemies if they are divided among themselves."

Moral: Even those who appear to be enemies can sometimes become benefactors if treated with kindness and understanding.
