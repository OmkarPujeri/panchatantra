The Gullible Carpenter

In a small village, a carpenter lived with his wife. People in the village spoke ill of her, saying she was unfaithful.

The carpenter, wanting to test her, thought of a plan. He told her he would be away for several days and asked her to prepare lunch. His wife was nervous, but she quickly made a delicious meal, thinking she would be free to do as she pleased.

The next day, the carpenter left for the forest, but instead of going far, he hid under the bed in his own house. His wife, thinking she was alone, met her lover and invited him to stay. But as the lover was about to join her in bed, she accidentally stubbed her toe on the carpenter's body.

Thinking it was the carpenter testing her, she came up with a clever plan. She told the lover that a goddess had appeared to her. The goddess said her husband would soon die. If she embraced another man, the death would pass to him. Then her husband would live a hundred years. The lover, believing this, did as she said.

The carpenter, fool that he was, felt his heart thrill with joy when he heard her words. He came out from under the bed and called her a faithful wife. He said he had only pretended a trip to another village to test her, and had hidden under the bed. He even asked the lover to join him in a celebratory dance, claiming that the lover had helped him live longer.

The carpenter's relatives were shocked by his behavior, but he just laughed and said that he was happy to have a long life ahead of him. He danced around the village, with the lover still on his shoulder, much to the amusement of the villagers.

Moral: It's foolish to pardon someone who has clearly done wrong, and even more foolish to reward them for it.
