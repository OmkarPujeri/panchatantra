The Results of Education

Two chicks, born to a hen-parrot on a mountain, were caught by a hunter. One chick escaped, while the other was taken to a cage and taught to speak.

The escaped chick met a wandering holy man, who took him to his hermitage and cared for him. Meanwhile, the caged chick was taught to mimic the voices of its surroundings.

A king, whose horse ran away, entered the forest where the hunter lived. The caged chick, recognizing the king's approach, began to mock his voice, saying, "Bind him, bind him! Kill him, kill him!" The king, deceived by the chick's words, quickly changed direction.

Later, the king came across another hermitage, where a parrot in a cage greeted him warmly. The parrot invited the king to rest and offered him food and water. The king was puzzled by the parrot's kind words, which contrasted with the caged chick's cruel ones.

The king shared his experience with the parrot, telling him about the caged chick's threatening words. The parrot explained that its own life had been shaped by its education. It had grown up among holy men and learned gentleness. The other parrot had grown up among hunters and learned only cruelty.

The parrot's words were a lesson to the king: "Our education, whether good or bad, determines our actions. Association with evil can lead to evil consequences. It's wiser to avoid fools and befriend good people."

Moral: The company we keep and the lessons we learn shape who we become in life.
