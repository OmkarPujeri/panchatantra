The Unforgiving Monkey

In a certain city, King Moon had a pack of monkeys for his son's amusement. They were well-fed and happy.

One of the monkeys, a ram, had a habit of entering the kitchen and eating everything in sight. The cooks would beat him with sticks, but the chief monkey, wise and cautious, saw a bigger threat. "If the cooks get angry enough, they might use a firebrand, and the ram's woolly back will catch fire. The stable nearby is made of thatch, and if the ram burns, it will spread to the horses. And if the horses get burned, they'll need monkey-fat to heal. But we monkeys will be the ones to provide that fat, and we'll be the ones to die!"

The chief monkey gathered the other monkeys and warned them about the danger. "A quarrel between the ram and the cooks will mean our deaths. It's better for us to leave the palace and live in the forest."

But the monkeys laughed at the chief's warning. "You're old and your mind is slipping," they said. "We'll stay here and enjoy the good food the prince gives us."

The chief monkey sighed and left the palace, going to the forest to live. But he left behind a warning that would come too late.

One day, the ram entered the kitchen and the cooks, in a fit of rage, used a firebrand to beat him. The ram ran into the stable, causing a fire that burned the horses. The king was worried and asked his veterinary surgeons for a solution. They told him to use monkey-fat to heal the horses, but the king knew that meant the monkeys would have to die.

The king ordered the slaughter of the monkeys, and soon they were all dead. The chief monkey heard the news and was filled with sorrow.

As he wandered through the forest, he came across a lake with lotus flowers. He noticed that there were footprints leading into the lake, but none coming out. He realized that a monster must be living in the water, and he decided to drink from a lotus-stalk to avoid it.

But the monster, a man-eating fiend, saw the monkey and was impressed by his cleverness. The fiend asked the monkey what he wanted, and the monkey asked for the fiend's pearl necklace. The fiend agreed, and the monkey gave it to the king, telling him that he could get a similar necklace by bathing in a magical lake in the forest.

The king, greedy for more pearl necklaces, decided to go to the lake with his retinue. At dawn they reached the water, and the monkey told the king's attendants to plunge in together. They all entered the lake and were eaten by the fiend. The king waited, wondering why his men lingered. Then the monkey climbed a tree and called down to him. "Villainous king," he said, "a fiend in the water has eaten your attendants. My revenge for the death of my household is complete. Now go. I did not let you enter, because I remembered that you were the king. Thus you plotted the death of my household, and of yours." When the king heard this, he hastened home, grief-stricken, while the fiend, fully satisfied, rose from the water.

Moral: Greed and a desire for revenge can lead to destruction and chaos.
