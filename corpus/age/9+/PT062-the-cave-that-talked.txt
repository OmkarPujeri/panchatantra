The Cave That Talked

In a dense forest, a hungry lion named Rough-Claw roamed around, searching for food. His throat was parched from thirst and hunger. As the sun began to set, he stumbled upon a massive cave and decided to hide inside.

Presently, the cave's owner, a sly jackal named Curd-Face, arrived at the entrance and started singing, "Cave ahoy! Cave aho-o-oy!" After a brief pause, he continued, "Hello! Don't you remember our agreement? You were supposed to speak to me when I returned, and I would greet you. But today, you're silent. I'll go to that other cave instead."

The lion thought, "This cave usually greets Curd-Face, but today it's quiet because it's afraid of me. I'll call out a greeting, and Curd-Face will follow the sound, providing me with food."

The lion loudly called out a greeting, but the cave's echo amplified the sound, scaring other forest creatures. Curd-Face, meanwhile, escaped, saying, "Joy comes from knowing what to dread, and sorrow strikes the foolish. I've lived in these woods for a long time, but never heard a cave talk."

Curd-Face departed, leaving the lion behind. The lion's plan had backfired.

A wise old crow named Live-Strong was overjoyed at Red-Eye's departure. He thought, "Red-Eye's escape is a blessing for us. He was wise, while the others were foolish. I can easily defeat them now."

Live-Strong started secretly building a woodpile at the fortress gate, planning to set it on fire and destroy the owls. The owls, blinded by their trust, didn't notice Live-Strong's sinister plan.

Cloudy, the king of the forest, was delighted when Live-Strong reported that the enemy's cave was ready to be burned. Cloudy and his followers, each carrying a lighted fagot, flew to the gate and threw the fire on Live-Strong's nest. The owls, trapped inside, could not find their way out of the flames.

Moral: Never trust an enemy, however friendly he seems, and do not make one without cause.
