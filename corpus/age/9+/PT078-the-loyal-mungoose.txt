The Loyal Mungoose

In a small town, there lived a Brahman named Godly with his wife and a young son. His wife also took care of a mungoose like a son.

She loved her children dearly, but was wary of the mungoose, thinking he might harm her baby. "Mungooses are notorious creatures," she thought.

One evening, she tucked her son into bed and left for water, instructing her husband to guard the baby. But the Brahman left for begging, leaving the house empty.

While the mother was away, a black snake slithered into the house and headed for the baby's cradle. The mungoose, sensing danger, bravely attacked the snake and killed it.

Excited by his heroism, the mungoose rushed to meet his mother, but she saw his bloody mouth and misinterpreted the situation. She assumed the mungoose had eaten her baby and, in anger, dropped a water-jar on him, killing him instantly.

The mother rushed home, only to find the baby safe and the snake torn to bits. She was overcome with grief for her thoughtless actions and killed her loyal mungoose.

The Brahman returned home with food, but his wife was too distraught to explain the situation. She accused the Brahman of being greedy and responsible for the mungoose's death.

The Brahman was puzzled and asked his wife to clarify what had happened. She recounted the story of the mungoose's bravery and her own thoughtless actions.

Moral: Greed and impulsiveness can lead to tragic mistakes, causing harm to those who truly care for us.
