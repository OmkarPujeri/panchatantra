Shell-Neck, Slim, and Grim

In a certain lake lived a turtle named Shell-Neck. He had two friends, ganders named Slim and Grim. A twelve-year drought hit the land, and the ganders thought, "This lake has gone dry. We must leave."

They said goodbye to Shell-Neck, their dear friend. Shell-Neck asked, "Why do you bid me farewell? I'm a water-dweller. If you leave me, I'll die quickly from thirst and grief." The ganders replied, "We can't take you with us. You're a water-creature without wings."

Shell-Neck thought for a moment and said, "There's a way. Bring a stick of wood." They did, and the turtle gripped the stick between his teeth. "Now take hold with your bills, one on each side. Fly up and travel until we find another lake."

But the ganders had a problem. "If you talk, you'll lose your grip, and we'll drop you." Shell-Neck vowed, "From now on, I'll be silent, even in heaven." They carried out the plan, flying over a neighboring city. People below pointed and asked, "What's this strange object?"

The turtle, forgetting his vow, asked, "What's all the chatter about?" He lost his grip, and the people below cut him to pieces with sharp knives.

Moral: Even the most well-intentioned plans can fail if you can't keep your promise of silence.
