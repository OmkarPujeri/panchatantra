Gold's Gloom

In the city of Maidens' Delight, near a shrine to Shiva, lived a hermit named Crop-Ear. 
He would beg for food in the city and bring back delicious treats, jellified and flavored with sugar and pomegranate. 
Crop-Ear and his companions would eat the food, but a sneaky mouse kept stealing the leftovers from the alms-bowl.

Crop-Ear tried to outsmart the mouse by hanging the bowl higher and higher, but the mouse could jump and climb with ease. 
The hermit was frustrated and worried that the mouse would ruin his food.

One day, a holy man named Wide-Bottom arrived as a guest. Crop-Ear welcomed him warmly and offered him food and rest. 
As they talked, Crop-Ear's mind kept wandering back to the mouse problem, and he absent-mindedly struck the alms-bowl with a bamboo stick.

Wide-Bottom noticed Crop-Ear's distraction and grew angry, accusing him of being a bad host. 
He said that Crop-Ear's pride in his home had made him forget how to be a kind and welcoming friend.

Crop-Ear was terrified and explained the situation to Wide-Bottom. 
The mouse was stealing his food, and he was trying to scare it away by striking the bowl.

Wide-Bottom asked Crop-Ear if he had found the mouse's hole, but Crop-Ear said he hadn't. 
The holy man then explained that the mouse's hole was probably near its stash of food, and that the smell of the food was what made the mouse so active.

Crop-Ear was amazed by Wide-Bottom's wisdom and asked him to tell more stories.

Moral: A true friend looks beyond surface-level problems and sees the underlying causes of trouble.
