The Potter Militant

In a small village, a potter named Fight-Firm lived. One day, he rushed into a broken pot and fell, injuring his forehead. Although he was bleeding heavily, he struggled to get up.

Later, a severe famine hit the land, and Fight-Firm joined a group of guards to fight in another country. His bravery and strength earned him the king's attention.

The king noticed the ugly scar on Fight-Firm's forehead and thought he must be a great warrior. He gave him many honors and gifts, treating him like a prince. The other princes were jealous, but they kept quiet.

During a review of the army, the king asked Fight-Firm about his name, family, and the origin of his wound. Fight-Firm confessed that he was a potter who had gotten the scar from a broken pot when he was drunk.

The king was shocked and ashamed. He realized he had been fooled by Fight-Firm's story. He ordered his guards to punish Fight-Firm.

Fight-Firm asked the king not to punish him, but to let him prove his bravery in battle. The king refused. He said Fight-Firm might be brave and clever, but he had no noble blood. The king told him to leave before the soldiers learned he was a potter. So Fight-Firm left.

Moral: The heedless trickster who lets the truth slip out is sure to lose his victory. Fight-Firm had all the virtues, yet the king sent him away, for no elephants are slain in his family.
