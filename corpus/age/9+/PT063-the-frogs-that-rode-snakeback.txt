The Frogs That Rode Snakeback

There was once a lazy black snake named Slow-Poison who lived near a pond. He thought to himself, "How can I get along without using too much energy?" He decided to visit the pond and pretend to be sad.

A curious frog approached him and asked, "Uncle, why aren't you catching food like usual?" Slow-Poison explained that he was cursed by a Brahman's son. He said, "I was trying to catch a frog, but he escaped and hid among some Brahmans. I bit a Brahman boy's toe by mistake, thinking it was a frog. The boy died, and his father cursed me to become a vehicle for frogs, relying on them for food."

The frog told all the other frogs about Slow-Poison's story. They were thrilled and informed the frog-king, Water-Foot. He and his counselors climbed onto Slow-Poison's hood, followed by the other frogs in order of age. Some frogs hopped behind the snake, and Slow-Poison showed off his skills.

Water-Foot was delighted with the ride and said, "I'd rather ride Slow-Poison than any other vehicle." The next day, Slow-Poison moved slowly, and Water-Foot asked, "Why don't you carry us as usual?" Slow-Poison replied, "I'm hungry, but I can eat the smaller frogs." Water-Foot even suggested that Slow-Poison should eat the plebeian frogs.

Slow-Poison was overjoyed and ate the frogs, growing strong in a few days. He thought to himself, "The trick worked. Now I have the power to eat all sorts of frogs." Water-Foot still trusted Slow-Poison.

Another black snake, a huge fellow, arrived and was shocked to see Slow-Poison carrying frogs. He said, "Partner, this is disgusting! They're our natural food." Slow-Poison explained that he was just following the Brahman's curse. But Slow-Poison only hummed a little song and said nothing more. Water-Foot asked him what the song meant. Slow-Poison just smiled and told him, "Nothing at all." Water-Foot believed him and did not see the danger. Then Slow-Poison ate every frog in the pond. He ate them so completely that not even one tiny frog was left.

Moral: Sometimes, people take advantage of situations to get what they want, even if it means using others for their own benefit.
