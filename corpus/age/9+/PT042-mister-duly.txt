Mister Duly

In a bustling city, a merchant named Ocean lived with his son. One day, the son bought a book at a sale for a hundred rupees. The book contained a single line: "What's duly his, a man receives." Ocean saw the book and asked his son, "What did you pay for this book?" The son replied, "A hundred rupees." Ocean scolded him, "You fool! If you buy a book with just one line, how will you make money?" He then threw his son out of the house.

The young man wandered to another country, where he met many people who asked him about himself. He replied, "What's duly his, a man receives." This became his nickname, Mister Duly.

One day, a beautiful princess named Moonlight gazed out of her palace window and spotted a handsome prince. She immediately fell in love with him and sent her friend to invite him to meet her. The prince agreed, but when night fell, he changed his mind. He thought, "A man who tries to get into a teacher's child's house or a friend's wife's house will bring shame to himself and others. A deed that causes disadvantage should not be done." So, he didn't go to the princess.

Meanwhile, Mister Duly roamed the city at night, searching for a place to rest. He spotted a strap hanging from a house and climbed up. The princess welcomed him with great hospitality, but when he refused to talk to her, she kicked him out.

Mister Duly continued his journey and soon found himself in a temple. A policeman who was supposed to meet a woman arrived and saw Mister Duly sleeping. He asked him, "Who are you?" Mister Duly replied, "What's duly his, a man receives." The policeman, trying to avoid trouble, told Mister Duly to sleep in his bed.

However, the bed belonged to the policeman's daughter, Naughty, who had made a date with her lover. She woke up, mistook Mister Duly for her sweetheart, and married him. But after a while, she scolded him for not talking nicely to her and sent him away.

As Mister Duly walked through the city, he joined a procession of a bridegroom named Fine-Fame. But when an elephant ran amuck, the procession panicked and ran away. Mister Duly, however, stayed calm and saved the bride from the elephant.

The king, who had heard about the events, arrived and asked Mister Duly to explain. Mister Duly replied, "What's duly his, a man receives." The princess, the policeman's daughter, and the merchant's daughter all spoke up, saying that Mister Duly had indeed received what was due to him. The king was pleased and promised immunity to all involved. He even gave Mister Duly his own daughter and a thousand villages, and made him crown prince.

Moral: What's truly yours will be given to you in the end.
