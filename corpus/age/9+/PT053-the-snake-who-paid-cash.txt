The Snake Who Paid Cash

A Brahman spent his days farming, but his crops didn't flourish.

One hot summer day, he dozed in the shade of a tree, and a massive snake poked its hood out from an anthill nearby. The Brahman thought, "This must be the guardian deity of the field. I never honored him, so my farm work is unproductive."

He begged milk from a neighbor, poured it into a saucer, and approached the anthill. "Guardian of the field, I didn't know you lived here. Please forgive me for not honoring you." He offered the milk and went home.

Next morning, he found a gold coin in the saucer. He repeated this daily ritual, receiving a coin each time. One day, while the Brahman was in town, his son took the milk to the anthill, set it down, and went back home.

The next day, the boy found only one coin and thought, "This anthill must be filled with treasure. I'll kill the snake and get all the coins!" He struck the snake with a cudgel, but it didn't die. Instead, the snake bit the boy with its sharp fangs, and he died instantly.

The Brahman returned home, learned about his son's death, and realized what had happened. He said, "If we're not kind to all living beings, our lives will end like swans leaving their lotus nest."

Moral: Treat all creatures with kindness and respect to avoid losing everything.
