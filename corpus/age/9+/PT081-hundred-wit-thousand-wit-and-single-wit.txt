Hundred-Wit, Thousand-Wit, and Single-Wit

In a pond lived two fish, Hundred-Wit and Thousand-Wit, and a frog named Single-Wit. They enjoyed conversations at the water's edge, sharing witty remarks.

One day, fishermen came with nets, saying, "This pond has plenty of fish, and the water is shallow. We'll return at dawn." The three friends were shocked and took counsel together.

The frog asked, "Hundred-Wit and Thousand-Wit, what should we do? Flee or stay?" Thousand-Wit laughed, saying, "Don't be afraid. They won't invade. I'll save us with my tricks." Hundred-Wit added, "Thousand-Wit is right. We can't leave our home. I'll save you with my wit."

The frog disagreed, "I have only one wit, and it tells me to flee. I'm taking my wife to another pond tonight." And so, the frog left under cover of night.

At dawn, the fishermen came with nets, catching all the water creatures, including Hundred-Wit and Thousand-Wit. The frog, however, had escaped to a cistern.

The next day, the fishermen carried Hundred-Wit and Thousand-Wit away. The frog, safe in the cistern, said to his wife, "Look, my dear! Hundred-Wit is on a head, and Thousand-Wit is dead. Meanwhile, I'm swimming freely."

The frog's wife asked, "What happened?" The frog replied, "Hundred-Wit and Thousand-Wit trusted their cleverness, and it did not save them. Intelligence is not the only factor in fate."

Moral: Intelligence and knowledge are not enough to guarantee safety; sometimes, humility and listening to others are more important.
