The Musical Donkey

In a certain town, a donkey named Prig carried laundry packages by day, but roamed freely at night. One evening, while Prig wandered in the fields, he met a jackal and they became friends. They broke through a hedge into a cucumber garden, ate their fill, and parted at dawn.

That night, Prig stood among the cucumbers and said to the jackal, "The night is perfect for a song. What shall I sing about?" The jackal whispered, "Uncle, please don't. We're on a mission, and thieves and lovers should keep quiet. Your singing might attract the farmers, and they'll catch us."

But Prig insisted, "Come, come! You're just jealous of my musical talent. I know the rules of music: seven notes, three scales, twenty-one modulations, forty-nine pitches, and more. I can sing a hundred songs with perfect phrasing." The jackal tried to reason, "Uncle, your bray is harsh. Why not just eat in silence?" But Prig wouldn't listen.

The jackal reluctantly agreed to stay by the hedge, and Prig began to sing. The farmers, hearing the donkey's loud bray, rushed in, beating Prig and hobbling him with a mortar. As the donkey stood up, he forgot the pain, and with the mortar on his neck, he trampled the hedge and ran away.

The jackal watched with a smile and said, "Well sung, uncle! Why didn't you listen to me?" Prig realized his mistake and said, "You were right. I should have listened to you. My singing led to trouble."

Moral: He who lacks wisdom and doesn't listen to his friend will often end up in trouble.
