The Snake in the Prince's Belly

In a kingdom ruled by King Godlike, his son suffered from a mysterious illness. A snake had made its home in the prince's belly, and he felt weak in every limb.

The prince became dejected and left his kingdom. He went to a distant city, where he begged for alms in a grand temple. The people of the city were kind to him, and he spent his days in the temple's courtyard.

In the same city, there lived King Gift and his two young daughters. One of the daughters, named Princess Gift, always greeted her father with respect, saying "Victory, O King." The other daughter, however, was not as polite and often spoke harshly to her father.

King Gift was angry with his unpolite daughter and decided to give her away to a foreigner. He sent her to the temple with a few servants, where she met the prince. The princess was delighted to have a husband and went with him to a new city.

One day, while the princess was out shopping, she left the prince at home. She returned to find him resting on an ant-hill, and to her horror, a snake's head poked out of his mouth. Another snake crawled out from the ant-hill, and the two snakes glared at each other.

The ant-hill snake accused the belly-snake of tormenting the prince, while the belly-snake retaliated by pointing out the ant-hill snake's weakness. The two snakes continued to argue, each trying to outdo the other.

The princess, who had been hiding behind a tree, overheard their conversation. She quickly came up with a plan to get rid of the snakes. She made the prince drink black mustard, and then poured hot water into the ant-hill to kill the other snake.

The prince was cured, and the princess was hailed as a hero when she returned to her father's kingdom. She was highly honored and lived happily ever after.

Moral: When we work together and help each other, we can overcome even the most difficult challenges.
