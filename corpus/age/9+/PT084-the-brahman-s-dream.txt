The Brahman's Dream

In a small town, a Brahman named Seedy lived a simple life. He begged for some barley-meal, ate a portion, and filled a jar with the remainder. That night, he hung the jar on a peg, placed his cot beneath it, and gazed at the jar.

Seedy's mind began to wander, and he started thinking about the future. "If famine comes, I'll get a hundred rupees from this barley-meal," he thought. "With that money, I'll buy two she-goats, and they'll give birth to more goats every six months. Then I'll get cows, sell their calves, and buy buffaloes. The sale of buffaloes will bring me mares, and from those mares, I'll get many horses. I'll sell the horses and get a lot of gold. With the gold, I'll buy a big house with an inner court. Someone will offer me their daughter with a dowry, and she'll bear a son. I'll name him Moon-Lord, and when he's old enough to ride on my knee, I'll sit on the stable roof and think. Moon-Lord will jump from his mother's lap to ride on my knee, but he'll go too close to the horses. I'll get angry, shout at my wife to take the boy, but she'll be busy and ignore me. Then I'll get up and kick her."

Seedy was so lost in his dream that he didn't notice his foot flying up to kick the jar. The jar shattered, and the barley-meal spilled all over him, turning him white.

Moral: Indulging in hopes and daydreams can lead to accidents and foolish decisions.
