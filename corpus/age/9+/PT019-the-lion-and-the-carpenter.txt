The Lion and the Carpenter

In a forest, a carpenter named Trust-god lived with his wife. They spent their days cutting logs. A lion named Spotless, with a jackal and a crow as his friends, lived in the same forest.

One day, the lion met the carpenter. The carpenter, seeing the lion, approached him calmly and said, "Come, friend! I'll share my lunch with you." The lion, being carnivorous, said he didn't eat rice, but he'd try the carpenter's food. The carpenter fed him sweets and treats, and the lion was pleased.

To show his gratitude, the lion promised to let the carpenter pass through the forest safely. The carpenter asked the lion to come alone each day, but the lion's friends, the jackal and the crow, were jealous. They begged the lion to take them to the carpenter's house, where they could eat his food.

The lion told them he visited a friend who fed him delicious food, but he didn't want to take them. The jackal and the crow decided to kill the carpenter and take his food for themselves. The lion, however, felt guilty and said he'd get some food for them.

The three friends set out to find the carpenter. When they arrived, the carpenter and his wife were already in a tree. The lion explained that he was his friend, but the carpenter refused to come down, saying, "I don't trust your friends, and I'm not comfortable with them around."

The carpenter stayed up in the tree and would not come down. He did not trust the lion's friends, the jackal and the crow. He wanted nothing to do with them.

Moral: A king with shabby advisers brings no good to those who depend on him.
